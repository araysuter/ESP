# Representative vehicle parameters

These are approximate game inputs, not flight-certified models. Stage masses include simplified attached structures/payload allowances. The core uses a single constant Cd, fixed area, point mass, and pressure-interpolated thrust and Isp; it does not reproduce engine throttling constraints, mixture ratio, ignition timing, vehicle attitude dynamics, or operational trajectories. No orbit is claimed without an actual test. User modification changes these representative values.

## Starship / Super Heavy

The reference is the 124 m configuration on [SpaceX's vehicle page](https://new.spacex.com/vehicles/starship), not a claim that every 120 m prototype shares it. SpaceX lists Super Heavy 33 engines, 3,650 t propellant capacity and 8,240 tf thrust; Starship has six engines, 1,600 t propellant capacity and 1,614 tf thrust. These become 3,650,000 and 1,600,000 kg; 80.8 MN and 15.83 MN approximately. The upper-stage thrust is used as vacuum thrust; the booster published thrust is used as sea-level representative thrust. The 250 t booster dry mass, 150 t upper dry mass including payload, booster 88 MN vacuum thrust, upper 11 MN sea-level thrust and Isp values (320/350 s booster, 320/360 s upper) are **rough assumptions**, not published vehicle specifications. A real upper stage has three sea-level and three vacuum engines and cannot be faithfully described by a single pressure interpolation. Fixed 9 m circular reference area and Cd 0.5 are visual/game approximations.

## SLS Block 1 crew

[NASA's SLS reference guide](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/sls_reference_guide_2022_web.pdf) describes a 1,090 t fully fueled core excluding engines, four RS-25s, two five-segment solids, and an ICPS. The guide reports each booster at 726 t wet, 99.3 t empty, 16.014 MN maximum vacuum thrust and 126 s nominal burn; subtraction gives approximately 626.7 t propellant each. This is a maximum, not a constant measured thrust curve, and cannot simultaneously establish exact burn history or sea-level thrust. NASA lists the ICPS as 32.7 t fueled and 110 kN vacuum thrust. Approximate SLS modeled core dry 100 t / propellant 1,000 t and upper dry 30 t / propellant 27 t include rough engine, adapter and Orion allowances; they are **estimates**, not separate NASA mass statements. Core thrust 7.9/9.3 MN, Isp 366/452 s, and ICPS sea-level thrust 0, Isp 400/450 s are **representative assumptions** requiring refinement against engine data. The ICPS is an in-space engine; zero sea-level thrust does not certify low-altitude operation. Cd 0.5 and cross sections are approximations.

`BuildSlsStages` returns only sequential core and upper stages. SLS cannot lift correctly with that sequential representation alone: both SRBs must ignite and burn **in parallel** with the core, contribute their wet mass and thrust, and be dropped after burnout. `BuildSlsBoosterParameters` returns an aggregate pair for the parallel booster model. Its constant vacuum-maximum thrust and Isp inferred from mass and 126 s are crude approximations, not a measured thrust curve. Do not label an SLS launch as representative until that parallel model is integrated and tested. Solid motors cannot be treated as throttleable like liquid engines.

## Integrated verification

The representative presets and parallel solid model passed full pad-to-orbit tests on the macOS ARM VM with the local .NET 8 SDK using isolated retargeted test projects. The guidance uses flatten altitude/exponent 220 km/2 for Starship and 120 km/3 for SLS, through ordinary controls. At 20/10 ms steps, Starship cutoff was 469.32/469.32 s at LC-39A and 468.72/468.72 s in South Texas. SLS cutoff was 1049.68/1049.61 s and 1044.72/1044.66 s respectively. All eight runs completed 60 s of coast above a conservative 200 km bound. This validates the game implementation under its assumptions, not the real vehicles. The Starship preset is not yet updated to a verified V3 hardware specification.

Packaged macOS ARM verification: Starship reached verified orbit at 529.36 s / 238.9 km; SLS at 1109.32 s / 390.3 km, after the required 60 s unpowered coast. Both passed map/site-change reset. NASA metadata was stale at test time and both runs explicitly used the frozen Open-Meteo profile valid 2026-11-14 21:00 UTC. This is not a claim of a current NASA-driven run or exact real-vehicle trajectories.
