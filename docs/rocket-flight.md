# Rocket flight core

The pure C# core advances an Earth-centered inertial position and velocity in SI units with a fixed-step RK4 integrator. Mission time zero aligns the inertial and Earth-fixed axes; Earth rotation is the simplified constant angular rate used by `EarthFrames`. WGS84 geodetic coordinates define the pad and local east/north/up directions. Atmospheric wind is transformed from ENU into the same inertial frame as velocity, and drag uses velocity relative to rotating air. Gravity is a point-mass approximation. Unity rendering must subtract a double-precision camera origin before converting coordinates to floats; scene transforms are not flight state.

`StageDefinition.DemoStages()` supplies a fictional two-stage vehicle, not specifications for a real rocket. Thrust and specific impulse interpolate between sea-level and vacuum values by ambient pressure; mass flow follows thrust divided by specific impulse and standard gravity. The fixed-step integration splits at fuel depletion, and manual staging drops the active stage and its remaining propellant without changing position or velocity. Steering targets are pitch above the local horizon and heading clockwise from north; actual angles change at a limited rate. The pad hold consumes fuel. Liftoff checks the vertical thrust component against local normal gravity; the hold test omits centrifugal relief and checks a mass-driven threshold again on the next tick.

Impact is detected against a surface at the pad's WGS84 geodetic altitude, not terrain. Impact time is linearly interpolated within the fixed step and the vehicle is placed on the rotating reference surface. This is an approximation, especially at large steps. The weather profile is a frozen vertical column, not changing weather along the trajectory; missing or expired weather uses standard atmosphere as described in the weather documentation. Drag is a simplified constant-coefficient point-mass model. There are no attitude dynamics, lift, structural limits, winds aloft varying horizontally, or simulated stage debris.

`OrbitDiagnostics` reports instantaneous point-mass osculating elements. Its `MinimumWgs84AltitudeBoundMeters` subtracts Earth's WGS84 semi-major axis from perigee radius: it is a conservative lower bound, not an exact geodetic perigee or proof of a sustained orbit. AssistedAscent commands an eastward pitch program through the same throttle, steering and staging controls as manual flight. It shuts off above 120 km once the conservative perigee bound reaches 200 km. Mission.Tests then requires 60 seconds of unpowered coast with that bound above 180 km.

The .NET 9 workflow runs the flight, atmosphere, frame, rocket and mission console checks, plus an explicitly historical offline GEOS force probe. The integrated checks passed on the macOS ARM VM using its .NET 8 SDK (isolated test projects retargeted from net9.0); exact .NET 9 CI is checked separately. At 0.02 / 0.01 seconds, cutoff was 420.700 / 420.690 seconds at Starbase and 421.160 / 421.150 seconds at LC-39A. LC-39A with the historical NASA GEOS fixture cut off at 421.140 / 421.130 seconds. All six runs stayed above a 200 km conservative bound during the 60-second coast; the test thresholds are 0.1 seconds cutoff convergence, 2 km perigee convergence and a 180 km coast bound. These are headless simulation results, not packaged visual verification. The historical probe is a force calculation at selected altitudes, not a trajectory or current weather.
