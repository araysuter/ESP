# Reentry visual references and limits

Reviewed upstream [KSP-RO/RealHeat](https://github.com/KSP-RO/RealHeat/tree/7fd548d7f7e722d8fa5cc23952094cc575cfad30) at commit `7fd548d7f7e722d8fa5cc23952094cc575cfad30`. Its [readme](https://github.com/KSP-RO/RealHeat/blob/7fd548d7f7e722d8fa5cc23952094cc575cfad30/RealHeat/Readme_RH.txt) identifies its license as `CC-BY-SA` without a version. Confirm the exact applicable terms with upstream before copying or redistributing any RealHeat material; no RealHeat files, code, configuration, parameter values, or art are included in this effect.

RealHeat describes a heating model rather than a temperature model: it considers atmospheric composition and velocity for shock temperature and adjusts density-dependent behavior. Its readme also describes an aeroFX density adjustment intended to reduce ascent flames while retaining strong effects lower in the atmosphere. These are conceptual references, not evidence of a validated plasma image or a direct URP port.

ESP's `ReentryPlasma` is original procedural mesh and shader code. It uses separately supplied air-relative speed, density and local flight direction to fade an artistic windward glow and wake; its thresholds, color, opacity and smoothing are artistic choices, not RealHeat's equations or measured heat flux. It does not simulate temperature, ionization, material response or heat damage. The first packaged capture at 60 km showed insufficient brightness; the revised response and broadside wake require fresh rendered qualification.

The tower animator is also a presentation approximation. The imported arms are separate meshes with estimated visual pivots; the nominal gap near the vehicle is centered around the launch mount at roughly 71 m above the imported tower origin. Geometry alone does not verify contacts, clearances, catching loads or an actual Starship procedure. Mission logic must determine whether capture succeeds or fails from the simulated approach; visual arm motion does not authorize or establish capture. Verify placement, moving clearances, reset and failed as well as successful attempts in the target player.

The Unity adapter runs trajectory prediction on one cancellable worker that owns only private flight-core clones. It holds mission time and displays the planning state; the main thread consumes the complete plan after task completion. Reset cancels the old planner. High warp keeps the same 0.02 s integrator and is allowed on unpowered return coast only; after deorbit it clamps to 20x at 130 km before atmospheric entry.

Development-only `-espReentrySmoke` runs a real pad-to-orbit-to-return rehearsal, records the actual return state/frozen weather fixture, captures entry at several altitudes, and requires a catch plus reset. `-espReentryReady` instead stops the automation after a real verified orbit and leaves normal controls available for interactive QA; it never fabricates an orbital checkpoint.
