# Night lights imagery

The bundled `BlackMarble2016Lights.png` is derived from NASA's [2016 Black Marble 3 km grayscale geographic TIFF](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/esd/eo/images/imagerecords/144000/144897/BlackMarble_2016_3km_gray_geo.tif). Credit: NASA Earth Observatory images by Joshua Stevens, using Suomi NPP VIIRS data from Miguel Román, NASA GSFC. See NASA's [description of the 2016 composite](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/90008/night-light-maps-open-up-new-applications) and [image use policy](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/).

The source is a historical global composite, not current lighting or a street-level map. Its grayscale is a visual background, not calibrated radiance in this application. The warm tint and twilight fade are display choices. No terrain or collision data is inferred from the image.

The source TIFF is 13,500 × 6,750 pixels (SHA-256 `3c1bc09978fa6a7f2c65453460fbd290b66441389f1135c111d699816b59b926`). It was resized to 4,096 × 2,048 with macOS `sips -s format png -z 2048 4096 INPUT --out OUTPUT`, without a manual threshold or brightness curve. The PNG SHA-256 is `f41cf76466ce4d6c59f91fe9aa9e9ec9c433349ccf1ab11177656b7f79134c49`. This discards fine spatial detail; the source remains obtainable from NASA. Longitude runs west to east across the image; north is at the top. A visual atlas inspection found recognizable North and South American, European, Nile, Indian, East Asian, and Australian patterns against dark oceans. This does not replace in-game geographic registration and day/night validation.

## Rendering qualification

A macOS ARM development player built with Unity 6000.6.9f1 imported the atlas as non-readable linear BC4, compiled all night-light shaders, and passed the bounded `-espNightLightsSmoke` review. Packaged 1440×900 captures show correctly registered North American coasts/Great Lakes, Europe/Nile, East Asia/Japan, and Australia; the paired daylight views suppress city emission and the Asia view shows the twilight transition. Offline and downloaded launch ground, an actual simulated ascent above 150 km, and return to the live UTC clock after reset were exercised. The 4K atlas becomes visibly soft at low-altitude flight distances; it represents regional observed brightness, not individual lamps or streets. Windows rendering remains untested.

## Observed clouds and night transmission

The Clouds view uses the latest available NASA GIBS MODIS Terra cloud-fraction observations, blended over up to three advertised dates. The UI retains the date range, fetched time, observed coverage, and missing-data status; this is a dated satellite composite, not instantaneous weather. Surface and Satellite views remain independently selectable. Launch wind/pressure/temperature profiles are separate NASA GEOS-FP or Open-Meteo data, with freshness checks and a labeled standard-atmosphere fallback.

Cloud fraction controls a visual opacity approximation, not measured optical thickness. At full night, fully observed full-fraction cells use 85% opacity, leaving 15% of underlying city emission; partial fractions attenuate less, and clear or unsupported cells remain transparent. A small 0.006 nighttime readability fill replaces the former 0.30 white fill. It is not measured moonlight, and no city illumination or cloud observations are invented. Daytime cloud shading is preserved.

The representative 10 km shell renders its outer face from above and its inner face from below, never both hemispheres in one view. Local terrain tiles use the same observed coverage, cloud color, and transmission when viewed from above the shell, preventing the foreground terrain camera from hiding clouds. Their geographic-column sampling and 9.5–10.5 km transition are display approximations; they do not model cloud-base height, optical depth, or volumetric parallax. Below the cloud layer, light from nearby ground does not pass through the overhead clouds and is not attenuated by them.

The packaged cloud review on 2026-11-15 at 03:00 UTC fetched actual NASA observations for November 13–15: 2,093,314 valid cells, 3,838 missing cells, and 99.969% area coverage. Matched night/cloud-only/clear-reference captures confirmed transmitted city lights and dark oceans; day, twilight, below-layer, 12.9 km, and 152.5 km views were exercised. The Kennedy below-layer view was mostly smooth sky, so it is not evidence of detailed local cloud forms. Coarse terrain tile boundaries remain visible around 12 km. The same run rejected stale GEOS-FP data and selected a fresh ten-level Open-Meteo profile valid at 03:00 UTC, froze it at launch, then verified the explicitly labeled offline fallback and reset. These are dated qualification results, not promises about future provider availability.
