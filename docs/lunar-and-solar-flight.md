# Earth, Moon and Sun flight model

The player can manually fly across the Moon's sphere of influence, leave Earth's sphere of influence, orbit the Sun, and impact the Moon or Sun. The existing assisted ascent remains an Earth-orbit aid; it does not plan or fly a lunar transfer. The authoritative spacecraft position and velocity stay in the existing double-precision Earth-centered inertial launch frame. Switching primaries changes the gravity model, not the spacecraft state or its frame. The active primary is selected by distance to the moving Moon (66,100 km SOI), then Earth (924,000 km SOI), otherwise Sun. These fixed spherical SOIs are game approximations, not physical boundaries. In the Moon and Sun patches, the primary's geocentric acceleration is included in addition to relative two-body gravity. JPL gravitational parameters are used: Moon 4.902800118e12 m³/s², Sun 1.32712440018e20 m³/s²; Earth retains the existing parameter. See [JPL planetary parameters](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/astro_par.html) and [satellite physical parameters](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/phys_par/).

The bundled offline vectors are geometric ICRF Earth-centered states from the [NASA/JPL Horizons API](https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/horizons.html), generated by `python scripts/generate_celestial_ephemeris.py`. Moon samples are six hours apart, Sun samples one day apart, from 2020-01-01 through 2037-01-01 UTC, inclusive. Cubic Hermite interpolation uses both position and velocity. A one-week smooth transition at each end joins a [low-precision orbital-elements fallback](https://stjarnhimlen.se/comp/tutorial.html); outside the interval telemetry says “approximate ephemeris.” UTC is used in place of a full independent TT/UT1 conversion and the existing launch frame applies IAU 1976 precession and GMST. This is a patched-conic game model, not flight navigation or an n-body integrator; other bodies, relativistic corrections, tides and lunar gravity harmonics are omitted. The user-supplied initial date is the computer's UTC clock at launch, and mission time advances it.

The reference checks compare separately requested Horizons vectors from 2026-11-15 at 03:00 UTC for the Moon and 12:00 UTC for the Sun. On the tested Unity runtime, interpolated position differences were about 7 m and 51 m respectively, with velocity differences below 0.00001 m/s. These checks are examples, not a guaranteed accuracy bound across all dates. The approximate fallback differed by about 192 km at 2026-11-15 00:00 UTC; its position is not suitable for precision encounter planning. State vectors and radii are SI. Moon surface speed is measured relative to its center; lunar spin and terrain are not modeled. The Moon uses a 1,737,400 m radius sphere, the Sun a 695,700,000 m radius sphere; collision detects a segment crossing, and the existing impact damage toggle applies. Earth retains its WGS84/pad surface.

The map uses a finite-horizon gravity-only sampled projection: cyan Earth, amber Moon and purple Sun segments, with estimated SOI-change markers. Each patch is drawn around its primary's current location; because the bodies move during flight, neighboring body-centered patches can appear offset at a transition. It follows the same primary selection as the simulation but is an approximation: it ignores thrust and drag, has finite sampling and stops at its sample/time limits, so it is not a maneuver planner or a guarantee of an encounter. Earth-only near-orbit markers remain available. Higher warp rates are available for unpowered flight above 120 km, subject to existing work limits, and the displayed actual rate may be lower than requested. Numerical integration and collision checks still run at the fixed flight step. Manual pitch can range from −90° to +90° so the player can point below the local horizon; assisted ascent retains its Earth-orbit target. No lunar transfer or landing automation has been added.

## Imagery

`UnityProject/Assets/ESP/Resources/MoonSurface.jpg` is the 2048 × 1024 JPEG from NASA's [CGI Moon Kit](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720/) ([direct file](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a004700/a004720/lroc_color_2k.jpg)), assembled from Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter camera and laser altimeter data; visualization credit: NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio / Ernie Wright and the LRO instrument teams. NASA's [media usage guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/) apply. The image is bundled for offline rendering, imported as an sRGB, mipmapped, compressed texture with a 2048-pixel limit. It is visual color data, not elevation or collision terrain. The lunar globe orientation is a simplified Earth-facing presentation rather than an exact lunar libration model.

The code checks include JPL reference and midpoint samples, dataset boundary continuity, Earth and lunar SOI changes, impacts, an Earth launch comparison, and projected Earth escape/lunar encounter. The old rocket, booster, cheats and orbit projection suites were also run in the Windows Unity editor. Packaged execution and visual/manual lunar flight still require target-machine verification; the Windows machine's Application Control policy blocks locally built unsigned programs. macOS has not been tested for this change.
